## Gemini 4 Argon Global Launch: API, Pricing, and Official Test Boundaries

Google DeepMind announced in its official blog on October 13, 2026, that Gemini 4 Argon is officially open worldwide. Developers and enterprises can call the Gemini platform via AI Studio and the Gemini platform's API; Google AI Ultra subscribers can use it in Antigravity; and Gemini App and Search AI Mode are available to Ultra and Pro subscribers.

This release didn't follow the "put the paper first, wait for the interface" approach, but instead put the model, price, and entry point on the table all at once. For those who truly want to work with it, the only question remains: is the task it handles worth the price?

![Official Announcement: Global Launch and Subscription Portal](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/source-evidence-1-c41fc621.png)

### Now, who can use it and where to use it?

The official entry point is divided into several channels: developers take the Gemini API paid tier, which can build in AI Studio and Android Studio; enterprises use Gemini for business, API, and Antigravity; regular users can experience it in the Gemini App and AI Mode search starting that day, provided they subscribe to Google AI Ultra or Pro. Researchers and cybersecurity defenders have separate application channels, which will be discussed separately later.

## Pricing and billing: Listing price, cache discount, and expiration time

![Official Announcement: Release Price and Expiration Notes](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/source-evidence-2-47a85571.png)

Argon's launch price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens charged at a 95% discount. Here, tokens can be roughly understood as the minimum billing unit for model read/write, similar to courier charges per package: the prompt you send is input, the model returns output output, and pricing is separate.

![AI Mechanism Illustration](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/official-release-mechanism-landscape.png)

Note that this price tag is "introductory." The official footnote states that after the introductory period ends on November 30, the price will apply at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, and this price applies only to the publicly accessible Argon model. In other words, the price seen now is not a long-term commitment; the call cost will directly double before expiration.

## Officially listed engineering and research cases

The official announcement devoted most of its coverage to actual tasks rather than just benchmarking. Several cases with clear numbers include:

![Official Announcement: Chrome and ADK Optimization Cases](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/source-evidence-3-7693fd02.png)

- The Chrome team used Argon to separate core web processing from browser function code, involving a legacy directory of about 6 million lines in Chromium's roughly 40 million lines of codebase. The official statement compresses the architecture refactoring that would have taken months into weeks.
- Google's open-source Agent Development Kit (ADK) team has enabled Argon to autonomously optimize overnight in Antigravity, reducing memory preparation time for long sessions by 40%, and improving cold start import speed by 21%. Changes have been made available in the google/adk-python repository.
- After Argon optimization, the open-source quantum chemistry package xTB has achieved a maximum speed of 2.3 times faster molecular structure prediction and 1.8 times faster conformational search. The changes have been submitted to the official xTB GitHub repository.
- Google used Argon on the A100 to increase Gemma 4 E2B's end-to-end inference speed by 2.85 times through vLLM, increasing throughput from about 37,900 tokens/second to over 108,000 tokens/second. The official claim maintains 100% token consistency with no quality degradation.

These cases all come from Google itself or early partners, are official reports, not third-party retests. They show that Argon produced quantifiable results under specific codebases, specific hardware, and specific task settings, but it cannot be directly extrapolated as "any old project can turn one week into three weeks."

![Official Announcement: Vals Index and ERQA Scores](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/source-evidence-4-90d1ef0c.png)

## The official evaluation results are as cited

On the official blog's list, Argon ranked first on the Vals Index, which measures models in key economic areas such as finance, programming, law, and taxation; On the ERQA benchmark, which tests physics-spatial reasoning in 3D environments, it scored 87%, officially outperforming other cutting-edge models; On the Gray Swan Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) benchmark, the success rate of 15 adaptive attacks was reduced to 0.7%.

To be clear: these figures are the results relayed in the official announcement; this article does not independently reproduce them, nor does it verify sample versions or scoring rules for each list. They can serve as reference signals for selection, but should not be regarded as the final conclusion of fair evaluation.

## Safety and alignment measures

![Official Announcement: Researcher and Defender Application Channel](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/2026-10-15-official-1dd915a82f3f/source-evidence-5-c1420012.png)

Google made the Security Stack one of its release priorities. Officially, it evaluated Argon's capabilities under the Frontier Safety Framework and deployed a defense layer combining jailbreak resistance, monitoring, and execution; In external creature red team tests, less than 1% of creature subtasks could be unlocked. For dangerous requests, Argon's violation rate on the adversarial test set was nearly halved compared to the previous Gemini model.

Regarding proxy alignment, the official statement states that Argon is trained to respect system boundaries, truthfully report negative results, and pause for human confirmation before taking irreversible steps; On programming benchmarks where the model does not retain motivation, shortcut attempts have dropped to 2%. At the same time, Google has built an automatic misalignment monitor that reads step-by-step reasoning and actions from agents in real time, capturing and preventing unauthorized workarounds.

## What can be done now, and what is the next step for acceptance?

For ordinary developers, the most direct acceptance path is: open AI Studio or the Gemini platform, use a paid API to run a real multi-step task, record the number of input/output tokens and actual time spent, then estimate costs against the release price. Don't just look at the official demo's 3D scenes or game prototypes; those are ideal demonstrations and don't prove your codebase can be compressed with the same cycle.

For researchers, Google is offering 10,000 free slots for the highest Antigravity usage tier for 6 months, with rolling reviews based on eligibility, capacity, and screening criteria. For cybersecurity defenders, the Fairwind Program offers Argon access without a network firewall, which also requires application. Neither of these channels is "registered and accessible" and is suitable for teams with clear research or defense scenarios.

The Gemini 4 Argon is just the first card in the Gemini 4 family. The official statement clearly states "this is just the beginning," and future models and pricing strategies are worth tracking. Currently, if you have complex code refactoring, long document analysis, or multi-step proxy tasks, you can conduct a small-scale validation based on the entry and cost standards mentioned above; If the task is simple and the call volume is low, the long-term cost after doubling the release price needs to be calculated first.
