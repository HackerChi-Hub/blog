## Practical operation: fill in the explanation, select usage, and continue editing after generation

- Open the AI section under "Create Animation" and paste a short piece of content you want to explain.
- Choose fullscreen, overlay, or continuous long scrolls, pick a suitable style, and clearly write the action and duration.
- Generate using the currently configured online model or LocalBrain; After the results enter the timeline, adjust the relationships between text, time, and visuals.
- Keep the original voice play, or add your own voiceover, images, and videos for unified preview and export.

You don't need to learn a whole animation programming language first. You clearly state "what to explain and which changes the audience should see," arrange objects and actions in the model, and the software draws the scene. The real convenience is that after the animation returns to the original project, you can continue editing without having to rebuild a video project just to add a brief explanation.

## After generation, can the footage continue to be edited?

The result of native animation is scene data: what text, images, and shapes are placed, when they enter, and how to move. It's more like a stage rehearsal sheet: each object has its own position and appearance time, and the software calculates it frame by frame. The animation is still a piece of material on the timeline, with adjustable position and duration; Internally supported layers and keyframes can be further modified, and after texting, the frame is recalculated.

![Native Animation | Drawing Editing and Transparency Overlay](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/hyphenscreen-ai-animation-150-preview/drafting.png)

This approach leaves preview and export to native synthesizers and does not require users to install a web animation development environment. Content, numbers, images, composition, and keyframes can be redesigned as explained; If the title is too long or the arrow blocks the button, adjustments can continue. **Style options are for production direction, not the built-in renderer for nine refined reference components. Currently, spatial and clay directions are mainly 2D expressions and cannot be understood as complete 3D modeling and lighting systems. **

![Native Animation | Continuous frame extraction in paper art direction](https://hyphentech.top/obsidian-assets/hyphenscreen-ai-animation-150-preview/paper.png)

The other is importing pre-rendered videos. These materials can be inserted in full screen, stacked, cropped, and reshuttered, resulting in higher image quality, but once in the editor, character models and lighting are no longer objects that can be adjusted layer by layer. When choosing a route, ask yourself: should you mainly replace text and data, or do you need complex materials and actions? The former is suitable for native scenes, while the latter can be turned into a video and then edited back to HyphenScreen.

## The animation returns to the timeline, with visuals and sound arranged together

This round also reorganizes the timeline usage relationships: visuals are understood as upper layers overlaying lower layers; images and animations need to be displayed on the recorded screen and arranged to higher levels; sound is understood as mixing, where spoken and background music can coexist and volume is adjusted separately. Adjustments like zoom and camera are separated from actual footage, allowing the audience to see what and how to display it in their own places.

Existing multiple-choice overall movement maintains the temporal relationship between objects, while empty track hiding reduces idle space. After adding animation, you still need to check the boundaries, occlusions, and volume; do not treat "already on the timeline" as a finished video. Screen recording is factual material, animation is explanatory material; both are placed in the same process and reviewed together at the end.

## How to use it now, what you save is your own time

- Screen recording tutorial: first carefully edit the real operation, then select one or two off-screen explanations to make animations. Keep the original voiceover to avoid retelling it for showy effects.
- Independent Commentary Clip: First, organize the script and materials, and generate one segment of footage according to a single meaning; Put animations, images, and dubbing back on the timeline for unified arrangement.
- Start with a short video: clearly state the object, actions, duration, and text, and have the model deliver a piece of content that can be accepted. After passing, proceed to the next segment.
- Must-watch after generation: check text, diagram data, occlusion, pause at the end, and sound; Mix refined videos with native scenes as needed.

My choice is to let generation serve interpretation: if the table needs to be checked by the audience, keep the real table; For relationships that are hard to show in screen recording, use animation to explain.
