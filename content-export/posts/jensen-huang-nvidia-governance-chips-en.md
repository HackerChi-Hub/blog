　　On November 8, Jensen Huang sent an open letter to NVIDIA employees, admitting "I was unfaithful to my wife," apologizing to family, colleagues, the board, partners, and shareholders, and expressing support for the investigation and full cooperation. ## $4.8 billion in equipment procurement, connecting private relationships to the global computing power business

　　The New York Times, citing ImportGenius's trade records, reported that from June 2024 to February 2026, Megaspeed's affiliates in Malaysia purchased at least $4.8 billion worth of advanced AI equipment containing NVIDIA chips and technology. What is truly worth understanding for ordinary readers is that this business is no longer happening only at customs. This viewpoint serves two things simultaneously: the continued expansion of the U.S. technology ecosystem, and Nvidia's continued market and CUDA advantages.

　　Nvidia's success has long relied on Jensen Huang's technical judgment, personal charisma, and external lobbying. The New York Times reports that he has more than 60 direct subordinates, and the company lacks a clear successor. When things are going well, this concentration brings speed and execution; But when private credit, customer investigations, and policy conflicts arise together, it becomes a single point of failure.

　　In 2018, Intel CEO Brian Kerzanich resigned due to a mutually agreeable close relationship with employees. This old case shows that corporate governance rules do not lose meaning just because one person has made great contributions. Jensen Huang faces an even more complex situation, as Nvidia stands at the crossroads of global chip regulation and AI competition.

　　The technological form is also ahead of regulation. Hardware, CUDA, overseas data centers, and remote leasing have already formed a new supply chain, with rules still supplementing the jurisdiction of remote access. Jensen Huang's departure will not change this contradiction; his successor will still have to deal with the same set of technology, customers, and policies.

　　Bloomberg commented that "China has become the biggest loser," a statement that has turned complex industrial competition into a winning slogan.
