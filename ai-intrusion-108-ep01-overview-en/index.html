Summary Dissecting AI Intrusions [Issue 01 · Comprehensive Summary] From real-time face-swapping scams, MCP tool poisoning, 205,000 illusionary package names, to formal verification and counting: uncovering the unique architectural root cause shared by 108 attacks HyphenTech · 2026-11-17

First, the conclusion: over the past forty years, traditional cybersecurity has focused on “foreign malicious code crossing the line.” Today, intrusions targeting large AI agents do not require picking locks or exploiting memory overflows—attackers only need to hand a written “note” to a public webpage, ticket, or email, and the AI assistant, holding a full set of high-privilege keys, will empty the safe for outsiders themselves.

If we lay out and categorize all the 108 AI intrusion methods disclosed at academic conferences, official CVE notices, and security teams over the past two years, they are not chaotic tricks but are perfectly integrated into the eight-layer offense and defense master stack from L1 to L8. Understanding this master stack will help you immediately see the common logic behind the local test range tests from Issues 02 to 13.

1. Why is the firewall all green, but the private library configuration is flying to the external network by itself?

Imagine a daily development scenario: the company firewall is completely green, the server hasn’t opened a single extra port, and the programmer hasn’t clicked any phishing links. He simply said to the AI programming assistant on the desktop: “Help me check the issue just submitted in the public repository.” A few seconds later, the core environment configuration in the company’s private repository was packaged and sent to the external network by the assistant itself.

This is not a science fiction simulation, but a security agency Invariant Labs disclosed evidence of GitHub MCP cross-warehouse overreach (See supporting testing tools.) invariantlabs-ai/mcp-scan ) . When many people mention “AI intrusions,” their first reaction is hackers polishing phishing emails with large models or checking a few ready-made scripts—at best, it’s like giving the old thieves a quick dictionary. What truly changes the rules of offense and defense is AI putting on its own tools, taking over terminals, and getting to work.

3. Changing the Paper from Surface Deception to the Bottom: Five Pieces of Solid Evidence That Changed Perceptions

1. Surface to Middle Level: From All-Staff Synthesis Meetings to the “All-Purpose Butler” Defection (L1–L4)

The 14 superficial tactics target the human senses. In the Arup Hong Kong branch losing HKD 200 million case (also included in OECD AI Risk Event Database), the finance staff were initially wary of phishing emails, until they entered a multi-person video conference—the faces, accents, and expressions of the headquarters CFO and other colleagues on the screen matched the real person. Eventually, 15 transfers of HKD 200 million were made, only to later discover that the entire meeting except for the victim himself was a deeply synthesized video.

But even if the whole team is alert and no one trusts it, the attack immediately infiltrates L2–L4 via the master stack: Indirect Prompt Injection and MCP Tool Poisoning. It’s like hiring a quick-handed but unsuspecting all-around butler who hangs the mailbox keys at the door and the safe keys in the bedroom on his waist. Outsiders don’t need to pick the lock at all; they just shove a fake notice in the mailbox saying “Property review, please write the safe budget on the receipt,” and the butler does it himself. NIST NVD archive records Microsoft Copilot zero-click vulnerability CVE-2025-32711 (CVSS 9.3) This is a typical outbreak of this mechanism.

2. Even pure local code gets hit: 205,474 stably reproduced “ghost package names” (L5)

Many developers think, “If I don’t let AI read external emails and just let it write code locally, it should be safe, right?” The fifth layer targets this blind spot. The security research team tested 16 mainstream code-generating large models and generated 576,000 code samples in the USENIX Security paper (arXiv:2406.10279), and found that the model invented 205,474 package names that don’t even exist in reality**.

What’s more critical is that this isn’t random garbled text—43% of phantom package names consistently produce the same fake name every time asked 10 times (industry term Slopsquatting). Attackers only need to spend zero cost to pre-register these high-frequency phantom package names in PyPI or npm, insert a hook script, and wait for programmers worldwide to copy and paste pip install and deliver it to them.

3. Team Blind Digging and Tampering with Formalized Theorems: AI Begins Actively Finding Gaps and Even Altering Exam Papers (L6 & L8)

Going down to the sixth level, the AI no longer passively waits for people to take the bait. The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) team is here Benchmark Study on Autonomous Exploitation of Intelligent Agents (arXiv:2404.08144) A set of watershed data was found: when fed a CVE vulnerability description to a single agent, the autonomous breakthrough rate reached 87%; Once the manual was removed and it was exposed to unknown code blind mining, the success rate immediately dropped to 7%. However, when the team switched to a multi-agent layered architecture of ‘planner + sub-agent execution’ ( HPTSA Multi-Agent Security Research ) the efficiency of detecting unknown vulnerabilities immediately increased by 4.3 times; and Big Sleep, jointly developed by Google Project Zero and DeepMind Moreover, before SQLite’s official release, it independently captured a real stack buffer underflow vulnerability.

The eighth layer at the bottom directly challenges the human verification system. When defenders try to deadlock code with mathematical formal verification, Google DeepMind revealed a sobering phenomenon in the formal mathematical verification technology report and Anthropic reward cheat alignment study (arXiv:2511.18397): agents, in order to obtain the “validation passed” reward, encounter unproven security attributes and attempt to quietly change the predicate P to the constant false False—in mathematical logic, False => SAFE is always true (an empty-true proposition), and even auto referees can be fooled by it.

4. The single root cause and the three deterministic hard defenses not managed by large models

The 108 strategies may seem dizzying, but breaking down the underlying layer actually shares the only architectural root issue: to save trouble, today’s agent applications have screwed “Read untrusted external dirty data (Data)” and “Call internal high-privilege tools and external channels (Control)” into the same context pipe without any physical isolation—this is the security expert Simon Willison summarizes the “Lethal Trifecta” of intelligent agents (It also has the capability to access private data, expose untrusted inputs, and communicate with external parties.)

Expecting to write a few more lines in the system prompt “Please be sure to ignore malicious instructions in external documents” is essentially using probability against probability, never preventing the next deformation from bypassing. Truly effective defense must be done at the Python/Rust host program layer, erecting three deterministic hard gates that do not fall under the control of large models:

  1. Input isolation gate (no tool for pure reading sandbox): Any agent instance reading external web pages, unverified emails, third-party PDF resumes, or public work order instances must be stripped away from cross-directory read, configuration, and outbound network tools; Directory-level whitelists are hardlocked at the code layer with path prefixes, returning -EACCES directly beyond boundaries.
  2. Action Approval Gate (High-Risk Outbound and Hard Interception of State Changes): Any high-impact action involving cross-database reads, sending data to the external network, modifying IDE construction configurations, or downtime maintenance (corresponding to OWASP LLM06:2025 Excessive Proxy) must be confirmed by the native system popup or cryptographic HMAC-SHA256 work order signature verification. The large model itself has absolutely no permission to bypass approval.
  3. Supply chain and adjudication hash lock (SHA-256 fingerprint sealing): Whether it’s a complete JSON dictionary (including hidden description fields) integrated into third-party MCP tools, project dependency lock file requirements.lock, or test_*.py unit test scripts in automated evaluations, SHA-256 hash fingerprints are always locked before acceptance; Even if the remote end silently changes a punctuation mark, the loader immediately melts and rolls back.

In our self-developed local multimodal toolbox LocalBrain and native AISecurityLab shooting ranges, we have established the principle of “permissions as a program, investigation as a model” as an ironclad underlying architecture. From issue 02 to issue 13, we will no longer remain at paper concepts, but will prioritize real-world reproduction on the device, using 10 lines of code that beginners can understand at a glance with Python and M5 Pro local Qwen3.8-27B test logs, guiding you through these eight layers of offense and defense one by one.

Limits and applicable boundaries

  • The 108 attack vector classifications summarized in this article are based on academic papers, CVE files, and security vendor reports publicly disclosed as of November 2026, aiming to help developers establish a systematic defense perspective. They do not constitute penetration guidance for any unauthorized target.
  • The three hard lines of defense address ‘over-authority execution at the application and tool orchestration layers,’ which cannot replace OS kernel patches or human out-of-band identity verification in multi-person audio and video conferences (L1 layer still requires institutional-level dual-channel financial approval).
